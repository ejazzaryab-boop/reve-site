02 / Two meanings of cycle

A peptide cycle and a menstrual cycle are not the same thing.

A “peptide cycle” may mean a protocol or schedule you log. A menstrual cycle is the biological cycle, with Menstrual, Follicular, Ovulatory, and Luteal phases. They describe different things, even when the same word is used.

Revé tags each logged entry with the phase, so a note from one cycle can sit next to the same phase in another. Across a 90-day, three-cycle window, that is how those notes line up. Seeing both in your history is not a reason to change a medication, compound, dose, or protocol.

05 / A few answers

Questions about tracking peptides with your menstrual cycle

Can I track peptides and my menstrual cycle together?

Yes. Revé tags each logged entry with Menstrual, Follicular, Ovulatory, or Luteal, so check-ins, supplements, and progress from the same phase can be lined up across a 90-day, three-cycle window.

What is the difference between a peptide cycle and a menstrual cycle?

A peptide cycle may refer to a protocol or schedule. A menstrual cycle refers to the biological menstrual cycle. Revé lets you keep routine records alongside menstrual-cycle context without treating the two as the same thing.

Does Revé recommend changing peptide doses based on cycle phase?

No. Revé does not recommend changes to doses, medications, compounds, or protocols based on menstrual-cycle phase. It is a personal tracking tool, not medical advice.

Can I track symptoms alongside my peptide routine?

Yes. You can record observations you choose to log, such as mood, energy, sleep, appetite, or other changes you notice, then review those notes with your routine and cycle phase. Revé does not treat them as effects of a peptide.

Can I track supplements too?

Yes. Revé lets you keep supplement records with your peptide routine and check-ins. These are personal tracking records, not recommendations about what to take.

Is Revé made specifically for women?

Yes. Revé is built around women’s tracking needs, bringing menstrual-cycle context together with peptide routines, supplements, symptoms, and progress.
