Notes for your clinician

Writing a few lines before a visit helps you remember what you wanted to say. Keep the facts you logged separate from the questions you still have, and leave the interpretation to your clinician. You can print the sheet blank and fill it in by hand.

Facts in one place. Questions in another.

Put the visit date and the date range at the top, then the names as they are written on your own labels. Under that, changes you may want to mention, with the cycle phase or cycle day they fell in. 02 / From the log you already have

How to fill it in from your log.

You are copying a slice of a record you already kept. You are not building a new plan on the sheet, and you are not guessing at blanks to make it look finished.

01

Line entries up by cycle day or phase

Put the same phase beside the same phase: menstrual with menstrual, follicular with follicular, ovulatory with ovulatory, luteal with luteal. A calendar week can split a phase, or mix two of them. The boxes on the sheet keep that mix out of the visit notes. Use the cycle day you already use. If you do not use one, leave it unknown.

02

Bring only the date range for this visit

Write the first and last date you actually want to discuss. Older notes can stay in your log. If you want one earlier cycle for comparison, name it on its own line. Do not copy every week you have ever written down. A shorter range is easier to talk through.

03

Mark unknown as unknown

If you do not know the day, the phase, or what you logged, write unknown. A guessed phase will not match the rest of the sheet. An empty question line is fine. You do not have to fill every box.

Read a sentence before you place it. If it states what you logged, it belongs with the facts. If it asks what something means, it belongs on the numbered lines. The sheet will not answer the question. Your clinician will.

Copy names the way they appear on your own label or prescription. If your log uses a shorter name, write the label name beside it so you are both looking at the same words. This page does not list names, and it does not suggest what belongs in the log.

A change goes in the phase box for when you noticed it, with the day it started if you know the day. Write it as something you noticed. The sheet does not treat that note as an effect of a peptide, and it is not a reason to change what you logged.

The daily paper log, with a date, a cycle day, and a phase on each row, is the peptide and cycle log template. This visit sheet does not replace it. The fields you may choose to keep on an ordinary day are listed in what to track in a peptide journal. If you want a phase name for a date before you copy it here, the cycle phase calculator counts from a start date and a typical length. It is an estimate for labeling notes, not a fertility or contraceptive tool. Dates for measurements and progress photos can come from progress tracking by cycle phase.

03 / On iPhone

How Revé can help you fill the sheet.

Revé is a personal logging tool. It keeps what you enter. You can glance at your Revé history and copy what you want onto the sheet. Revé does not make a report for a clinician, and this page does not send the sheet anywhere.

Peptides and supplements

Log peptides and supplements with doses, timing, schedules, and reminders. On the sheet, write the name as it appears on your own label.

Check-ins you choose

Check-ins cover mood, energy, sleep, appetite, and changes you notice. Copy the ones you want to talk about. A change is something you noticed. Revé does not describe it as an effect of a peptide.

Measurements and photos

Measurements and progress photos are kept over time. Write down the dates you may want to mention. Progress photos are not tagged with cycle phase.

Phase on the record

Every dose, check-in, and measurement you log is tagged with your cycle phase. Compare those logs phase by phase and cycle to cycle. The tag is context. Revé does not tell you to change anything because of the phase. This page does not say supplement entries are tagged with cycle phase.

Open the dates you named at the top of the sheet, look at them, and copy by hand. Leave the rest in the app. More answers about Revé are in the FAQ.

A clear boundary

What this sheet is not.

Revé is a personal logging tool. This sheet is for organizing a conversation. It is not medical advice, a diagnosis, or a treatment plan. If you need urgent help, do not wait for an appointment.

The lines do not tell you what to take, what to change, or what a phase means. A change you write down is something you may choose to mention. It is not described here as an effect of a peptide. A phase name is a label for your notes. It is not a reason to change what you logged.

If your cycles are irregular or absent, a phase label may not line up from one cycle to the next. A clinician is the right person for questions about that. This page does not offer treatment advice.

A few answers

Questions about this sheet.

What is this notes sheet for?

It is a free page you can print and fill in by hand before you see a prescriber, pharmacist, or clinician. It keeps the facts you logged apart from the questions you want to ask. It does not interpret those notes. Nothing you write on the paper is stored by this page.

What should I bring to a peptide or prescriber appointment?

Bring the date range that matters for this visit, the names as written on your own labels, and any changes, measurements, or photo dates you may want to mention. Put your questions on the numbered lines. If you do not know a detail, write unknown. Your clinician interprets what you bring.

How do I line the notes up with my cycle?

Group what you logged by cycle day or by phase: menstrual, follicular, ovulatory, or luteal. The same phase beside the same phase is easier to talk through than a list sorted only by calendar date. A phase name labels the note. It is not a reason to change what you logged. A change you write down is something you may choose to mention. This sheet does not treat it as an effect of a peptide.

Can Revé fill this sheet in for me?

No. Revé is a personal logging tool. You can glance at your history and copy what you want onto the sheet. Every dose, check-in, and measurement you log is tagged with your cycle phase, so you can compare those logs phase by phase and cycle to cycle. Progress photos are tracked over time and are not tagged with cycle phase. This page does not say supplement entries are tagged with cycle phase. Revé does not make a report for a clinician, and this page does not store or send what you write.

Is this sheet medical advice?

No. Revé is a personal logging tool. This sheet is for organizing a conversation. It is not medical advice, a diagnosis, or a treatment plan. If you need urgent help, do not wait for an appointment.
