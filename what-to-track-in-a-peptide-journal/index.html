01 / How to keep a log

How to keep a peptide log without turning it into homework.

One entry a day is enough. The same fields, written the same way, are what make a later reading possible. Skip any line you do not want.

01

Write the day and the phase first

Put the date, the cycle day, and the phase on the entry before the rest. Those three are how you find the note later, next to the same point in another menstrual cycle.

02

Add the routine in your own words

If you log a peptide, write the dose you entered, the time, and the schedule you are following. That is a record of what you wrote down. It is not a dose to copy, and it is not a schedule to change.

03

Add check-ins, or leave them blank

Mood, energy, sleep, appetite, a change you notice, a measurement, or a progress photo can sit on the same day. Each one is optional. None of them is described here as an effect of a peptide.

02 / Fields for one day

What you may choose to log today

03 / Beside the entry

Note the cycle day and the phase next to what you wrote.

A date says when you wrote the note. Cycle day and phase give that note a second label, so a later menstrual cycle can sit beside this one.

Write the cycle day next to the date. Cycle day is the count you use inside one menstrual cycle. A common way to label a journal is to call the first day of a period day 1, then count forward. Use that only if it matches how you already count. The number organizes notes. It is not a test result, and it is not for contraception, fertility, or medical use.

Beside the cycle day, write one phase: Menstrual, Follicular, Ovulatory, or Luteal. Pick the name you are already using for that day. This page does not assign phases to calendar dates. The four names are labels for reading your journal later. They are not a reason to change a dose, a schedule, or anything else.

  • Menstrual
  • Follicular
  • Ovulatory
  • Luteal

Keep one more label from getting mixed in. A peptide cycle is a protocol or schedule. A menstrual cycle is the biological cycle, with a cycle day and a phase. The word cycle covers both, and they are not the same thing. If both appear in the journal, write them on separate lines.

On iPhone, every dose, check-in, and measurement you log is tagged with your cycle phase. That tag is context for the record. It is not a suggestion to change your routine. How a routine sits next to menstrual cycle context is on the peptide and menstrual cycle tracker page.

05 / How to read the journal

Look back phase by phase, then cycle to cycle.

A fair look puts the same phase beside the same phase. Menstrual with menstrual. Follicular with follicular. Ovulatory with ovulatory. Luteal with luteal.

Leave the phase where you wrote it

Use the phase name from the day you logged the note. Keeping the original label is what makes a later column mean the same thing.

Give it more than one cycle

One menstrual cycle is a single pass through the phases. Read more than one before you treat a difference as a pattern. The notes do not interpret the pattern for you.

Keep the fields steady

A comparison is easier when the same fields show up in the same way: the same cycle day habit, the same phase names, and measurements written the same way.

Keep the two cycles apart

A peptide cycle, a protocol or schedule, stays on its own line. The menstrual cycle stays in the cycle day and the phase. Do not fold one into the other when you look back.

Measurements and progress photos, read with the phase still on the measurement, have their own guide: progress tracking by cycle phase. Progress photos there are tracked over time. For the daily rows themselves, stay with the peptide and cycle log template rather than a second sheet on this page.

A few answers

Questions about a peptide journal.

What can I write in a peptide journal each day?

Date, cycle day, and a phase name: Menstrual, Follicular, Ovulatory, or Luteal. Then a routine line in your own words, and any check-ins you choose, such as mood, energy, sleep, appetite, or a change you notice. Leave a line blank if you have nothing to write. The list is optional.

How do I note menstrual cycle day and phase?

Write the cycle day you use for that date, and one phase name beside it. The names on this page are Menstrual, Follicular, Ovulatory, and Luteal. They organize the journal. They are not a reason to change a routine, and they are not for contraception, fertility, or medical use.

How do I look back phase by phase?

Read the same phase across menstrual cycles: menstrual next to menstrual, follicular next to follicular, ovulatory next to ovulatory, and luteal next to luteal. Give it more than one cycle before you treat a difference as a pattern. A peptide cycle, meaning a protocol or schedule, is not the menstrual cycle. Keep those labels separate.

Does Revé tag what I log with cycle phase?

Every dose, check-in, and measurement you log is tagged with your cycle phase. You can compare those logs phase by phase and cycle to cycle. Progress photos are tracked over time and are not tagged with cycle phase. Supplement entries can sit in the same routine. This page does not say they carry a phase tag.

Is a peptide journal medical advice?

No. This page is a personal logging guide. It does not recommend a peptide, a dose, a schedule, or a change based on cycle phase. Talk with a clinician about health decisions. Revé does not provide medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment.
