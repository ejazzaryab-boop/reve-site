Same phase against the same phase.

Put a measurement next to the same phase in another cycle, taken the same way. A number from a different week can belong to a different phase, so it does not sit in the same column of a fair comparison.

01 / Why the week can matter

Why the week of the cycle can matter for a measurement.

Measurements can shift across the menstrual cycle. Two numbers from different weeks are not always the same kind of comparison.

Some research has reported that self-reported fluid retention peaked on the first day of flow and was lowest in the mid-follicular period. That report is a prospective study of 765 cycles in 62 women (White et al., Obstetrics and Gynecology International, 2011, PMID 21845193).

Some research has reported that body mass, and in some groups waist and hip girths, differed between the follicular and luteal phases. That report is a study of 40 female athletes (Stachoń, Collegium Antropologicum, 2016, PMID 29139284).

Patterns vary between people, which is why your own records matter. A group finding is not a reading of your log. These papers do not say what a change means for you, and they do not name a day you should measure.

02 / A routine you choose

Use the same setup each time you measure.

The points below are choices for a consistent log. They are not health instructions, and they are not a schedule anyone is told to follow.

01

Same time of day, similar conditions

Choose a time of day you can repeat, and similar conditions when you can. Matching the setup makes later numbers easier to line up. The choice is yours.

02

The same measurements each time

Repeat the measurements you choose, and keep that list stable from one cycle to the next. This page does not tell you which measurements to take.

03

The same photo setup

If you take progress photos, repeat the light, the distance, the pose, and the clothing. Take them in the phase you want to line up later. This page does not name a best day for a photo.

03 / How to compare

Read one phase across cycles.

Line up the same phase, give it more than one cycle, and write down anything unusual in your own words.

Line up the same phase

Put menstrual next to menstrual, follicular next to follicular, ovulatory next to ovulatory, and luteal next to luteal.

Look past a single cycle

Look at more than one cycle before you treat a difference as a pattern. One cycle is a single pass through the phases.

Note what was unusual

If travel, illness, or a missed day is part of the story, write that in your own words. The note is there so you remember the context. It does not explain the number for you.

Keep the method steady

A fair column uses the same time of day, similar conditions, the same measurements, and the same photo setup you chose the last time.

A few answers

Questions about tracking by cycle phase.

Why compare the same phase instead of week to week?

Measurements can shift from one week of a cycle to another. Comparing those weeks can place different phases side by side. Lining up the same phase, cycle to cycle, and measuring the same way each time, makes the comparison fairer. Look at more than one cycle before you read a pattern.

Does Revé tag measurements with cycle phase?

Yes. Every dose, check-in, and measurement you log is tagged with your cycle phase.

Are progress photos tagged with cycle phase?

No. Progress photos are tracked over time. Revé does not tag progress photos with cycle phase.

Does Revé tell me what my measurements mean?

No. Revé does not interpret what a change means, and it does not say a routine caused a change. It is a personal logging tool. It does not provide medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment.

Can I use the printable without the app?

Yes. The grid works in this browser, and what you type is not uploaded. You can print it or save it as a PDF without the app.
