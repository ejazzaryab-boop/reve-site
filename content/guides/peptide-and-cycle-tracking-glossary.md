## Menstrual cycle terms

### Menstrual cycle

The span from the first day of one period to the first day of the next. Adult cycles commonly run about three to five weeks; Mayo Clinic puts typical bleeding every 24 to 38 days. Twenty-eight days is an average, not a rule, and the same person's cycle can vary month to month.

### Cycle day

The number of the day within the current cycle. The first day of period bleeding is cycle day 1, often written CD1.

### Menstrual phase

The days of period bleeding at the start of the cycle, when estrogen and progesterone are low. Revé labels these days Menstrual.

### Follicular phase

The part of the cycle from the start of the period until ovulation, when follicles in the ovary develop and estrogen rises. It is the most variable part of the cycle and the main reason cycle lengths differ. Many apps, Revé included, label the bleeding days separately as the menstrual phase.

### Ovulation

The release of an egg from the ovary, usually once per cycle. It happens closer to two weeks before the next period than on a fixed day after the last one.

### Ovulatory phase

The few days around ovulation, when estrogen peaks and a surge of luteinizing hormone triggers the egg's release.

### Luteal phase

From ovulation to the next period. Progesterone rises, then falls if there is no pregnancy, which starts the next period. Its length is steadier than the follicular phase.

### LH surge

A sharp rise in luteinizing hormone that triggers ovulation, usually a day or two before it happens. Urine ovulation tests detect it.

### Basal body temperature

Your resting temperature on waking. It rises slightly after ovulation because of progesterone, so it confirms ovulation after the fact rather than predicting it.

### Irregular cycles

Cycles that vary widely in length or fall outside the typical range. They are common with PCOS, in perimenopause, after stopping hormonal birth control, and with big changes in weight. Phase labels estimated from an average cycle length are less reliable when cycles are irregular.

### Perimenopause

The transition before menopause, often starting in the 40s, when cycles become less regular and symptoms such as hot flashes can begin. Menopause is confirmed after 12 months in a row without a period.

### PCOS

Polycystic ovary syndrome, a common hormonal condition that can cause irregular or absent ovulation, irregular periods, higher androgen levels and insulin resistance.

## GLP-1 and peptide terms

### Peptide

A short chain of amino acids. Some peptides are approved medicines, including semaglutide and tirzepatide. Many others are sold online labeled for research use only and are not approved for use in people.

### GLP-1 receptor agonist

A medicine that acts like the gut hormone GLP-1: it lowers appetite, slows stomach emptying and helps regulate blood sugar. Semaglutide is one.

### GIP

Glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide, another gut hormone involved in insulin release. Tirzepatide acts on both GIP and GLP-1 receptors.

### Dual agonist

A medicine that activates two receptors. Tirzepatide is a dual GIP and GLP-1 receptor agonist.

### Semaglutide

A GLP-1 receptor agonist sold as Ozempic (weekly injection for type 2 diabetes), Wegovy (for weight management) and Rybelsus (daily tablet for type 2 diabetes).

### Tirzepatide

A dual GIP and GLP-1 receptor agonist sold as Mounjaro (type 2 diabetes) and Zepbound (weight management), injected once a week. Its label tells people on oral birth control to use backup during the 4 weeks after starting and after each dose increase. See [the 4-week rule](/guides/mounjaro-zepbound-birth-control/).

### Titration

Starting a medicine at a low dose and raising it in steps, so the body can adjust and side effects stay manageable. Also called dose escalation. Tirzepatide's label raises the dose no sooner than every 4 weeks.

### Maintenance dose

The dose you stay on once titration is finished.

### Compounded medication

A drug mixed by a pharmacy for an individual patient. Compounded GLP-1 medications are not FDA-approved, and the FDA does not review them for safety, effectiveness or quality before they are sold.

### Subcutaneous injection

An injection into the fat layer just under the skin, usually in the abdomen, thigh or upper arm. GLP-1 pens and most peptide injections are subcutaneous.

### Injection site rotation

Changing where you inject each time, to reduce irritation and lumps under the skin from repeated injections in one spot.

### Half-life

The time it takes for the amount of a drug in the body to fall by half. Semaglutide's is about a week and tirzepatide's about 5 days, which is why both are taken weekly.

### Steady state

The point at which drug levels stay roughly level from one dose to the next. It is generally reached after about four to five half-lives on the same dose.

### Lyophilized

Freeze-dried. Many peptides are supplied as a lyophilized powder in a vial and must be mixed with a liquid before use.

### Reconstitution

Mixing a freeze-dried powder with a liquid to make an injectable solution. The amount of liquid added sets the concentration, which is why mistakes here lead to dosing errors.

### Bacteriostatic water

Sterile water containing a small amount of benzyl alcohol, which slows bacterial growth so a vial can be used more than once.

### Research use only

A label meaning a product is sold for laboratory research and not for human use. Research-use-only peptides are not approved medicines.

### Peptide cycle

In peptide communities, an on/off schedule for a compound, such as several weeks on and then weeks off. It has nothing to do with the menstrual cycle, and the shared word causes a lot of confusion in apps and search results.

### Washout

A period off a compound before starting another or restarting, so the effects of the first one fade.

## Logging terms

### Check-in

A quick log of how you feel, such as mood, energy, sleep or appetite, without a dose attached.

### Phase tagging

Stamping each log entry with the cycle day and phase it happened on, so entries can later be grouped by phase. Revé does this automatically for every dose, check-in and measurement.

### Phase-to-phase comparison

Reading the same phase across several cycles, such as luteal next to luteal, to see whether a pattern repeats. One cycle is rarely enough to call something a pattern.
