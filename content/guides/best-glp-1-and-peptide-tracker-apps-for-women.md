## Shotsy

Shotsy is the biggest dedicated GLP-1 tracker on iPhone. Its listing covers Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, Zepbound, Rybelsus, Saxenda, Victoza and Trulicity, including weekly injections and daily pills. AppGoblin estimates about 1.8 million installs and a 4.8-star average across roughly 35,000 ratings. A competitor's comparison page credits it with PDF export, multi-medication schedules and a maintenance mode.

**Good fit if** you want a proven shot log and side-effect tracker. **Less of a fit if** you want your cycle in the same view.

## MeAgain

MeAgain positions itself as an all-in-one GLP-1 app: shots, food (photo, barcode, voice), symptoms, weight and an estimated medication curve. Its site reports more than 400,000 users and a 4.8-star average from over 18,000 ratings, and it's the one app here that lists Android. It publishes pages on period and perimenopause symptom changes on GLP-1s.

**Good fit if** you want food logging and medication tracking together, or you're on Android.

## Back2Me

Back2Me is built for women on GLP-1 medications, including compounded versions. Its listing describes cycle tracking, symptom and side-effect logging tied to the cycle, and an insights dashboard.

**Good fit if** your main question is how your cycle and your GLP-1 side effects relate.

## Steady

Steady is a GLP-1 coaching app for women. Its listing focuses on protein and fibre targets, tracking your next dose, cycle tracking, and coaching through the week.

**Good fit if** you want nutrition targets and coaching more than a detailed log.

## GLP-1 Companion

GLP-1 Companion describes itself as an all-in-one tracker for women 40 and over on GLP-1 medications, covering doses, side effects and progress.

**Good fit if** you're 40+ and want an app pitched at that stage of life.

## PeptidePilot (and other peptide protocol trackers)

There are dozens of peptide tracker apps on the App Store. PeptidePilot is a typical example: a cycle planner, a vial locker, injection site rotation, a wellness journal and a clinician PDF, stored on-device with no account. Its "cycle planner" schedules peptide on/off periods. It does not track a menstrual cycle, and that's true of most apps in this group.

**Good fit if** you run multi-peptide protocols and want vial and site management.

## Revé

Revé is an iPhone app for women that logs peptides, supplements and GLP-1 routines with doses, timing, schedules and reminders, plus check-ins (mood, energy, sleep, appetite), measurements and progress photos. Every dose, check-in and measurement is tagged with cycle day and phase, so you can compare the same routine phase by phase and cycle to cycle. It's a free download, and full access needs a subscription.

**Good fit if** you take more than one thing and want to see whether it feels different in different weeks of your cycle. **Less of a fit if** you need food logging, Android, vial inventory, or a dosing calculator. Revé doesn't recommend doses.

## What to check before you pick

- **Your medication or compound is supported**, including compounded versions if that's what you use.
- **Cycle awareness.** Does the app know your cycle day on each entry, or do you have to cross-reference a second app?
- **Side effects and check-ins** are logged next to doses, not in a separate screen you'll forget.
- **Export** for your clinician, such as a PDF or CSV.
- **Privacy**: on-device only, or an account with cloud sync? Read the App Store privacy label.
- **Price**: free tier limits and subscription cost.
