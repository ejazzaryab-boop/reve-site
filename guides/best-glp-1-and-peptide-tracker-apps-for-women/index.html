Best GLP-1 and peptide tracker apps for women: November 2026

Ten apps, compared on what women on GLP-1s or peptides ask about most: does it handle my medication, does it know where I am in my cycle, and what does it cost. Revé publishes this page and is one of the apps listed.

By the Revé editorial team · Updated Nov 25, 2026

Short answer

There is no single best app. It depends on what you take and whether your menstrual cycle matters to how you read your logs.

  • GLP-1 shots only: Shotsy is the most widely used dedicated GLP-1 tracker (about 2.5 million installs and a 4.82-star average from roughly 50,000 ratings, per AppGoblin estimates re-checked November 23, 2026).
  • All-in-one with food logging, or Android: MeAgain.
  • GLP-1 plus your menstrual cycle: Back2Me and Steady are built for women on GLP-1s and include cycle tracking.
  • Peptides, supplements and GLP-1s, with every entry tagged to cycle phase: Revé (iPhone).
  • Peptides and GLP-1 medications on iPhone, stored on the device with no account: Protocol Her.
  • Peptides with cycle-phase context, listed as free on iPhone and iPad: Peptide Glow Up. Its listing does not mention GLP-1 medications.
  • Research-peptide protocols with vials and site rotation: PeptidePilot and similar tools. These plan peptide on/off "cycles", which are not menstrual cycles.

Shotsy

Shotsy is the biggest dedicated GLP-1 tracker on iPhone. Its listing covers Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, Zepbound, Rybelsus, Saxenda, Victoza and Trulicity, including weekly injections and daily pills. AppGoblin estimates about 2.5 million installs and a 4.82-star average across roughly 50,000 ratings (re-checked November 23, 2026). A competitor's comparison page credits it with PDF export, multi-medication schedules and a maintenance mode.

Good fit if you want a proven shot log and side-effect tracker. Less of a fit if you want your cycle in the same view.

MeAgain

MeAgain positions itself as an all-in-one GLP-1 app: shots, food (photo, barcode, voice), symptoms, weight and an estimated medication curve. Its site reports 1M+ downloads and 4.8 stars across 40K App Store ratings (re-checked November 23, 2026), and it's the one app here that lists Android. It publishes pages on period and perimenopause symptom changes on GLP-1s.

Good fit if you want food logging and medication tracking together, or you're on Android.

Back2Me

Back2Me is built for women on GLP-1 medications, including compounded versions. Its listing describes cycle tracking, symptom and side-effect logging tied to the cycle, and an insights dashboard.

Good fit if your main question is how your cycle and your GLP-1 side effects relate.

Steady

Steady is a GLP-1 coaching app for women. Its listing focuses on protein and fibre targets, tracking your next dose, cycle tracking, and coaching through the week.

Good fit if you want nutrition targets and coaching more than a detailed log.

GLP-1 Companion

GLP-1 Companion describes itself as an all-in-one tracker for women 40 and over on GLP-1 medications, covering doses, side effects and progress.

Good fit if you're 40+ and want an app pitched at that stage of life.

PeptidePilot (and other peptide protocol trackers)

There are dozens of peptide tracker apps on the App Store. PeptidePilot is a typical example: a cycle planner, a vial locker, injection site rotation, a wellness journal and a clinician PDF, stored on-device with no account. Its "cycle planner" schedules peptide on/off periods. It does not track a menstrual cycle, and that's true of most apps in this group.

Good fit if you run multi-peptide protocols and want vial and site management.

Protocol Her

Protocol Her is an iPhone app the App Store lists for women. The subtitle is "Women's GLP-1 Shot & Dose Log." The listing says it tracks peptides and GLP-1 medications, a menstrual cycle, side effects, and notes in one private journal, with no account and no sign-up, and that doses, photos, and notes stay on the iPhone. It also describes optional reminders, daily check-ins for weight, water, protein, energy, and appetite, a cycle log, read-only Apple Health for daily steps and nightly sleep if you connect it, measurements and photos, a place to record lab values for your own reference, vial and pen records with optional discard reminders, and a PDF of the logged history. The listing shows 4.8 out of 5 from 10 ratings. The download is free, with an optional subscription. It requires iOS 17.0 or later. The listing says it does not recommend doses. Checked November 23, 2026.

Good fit if you want logs stored on the iPhone with no account, Apple Health steps and sleep in the same view, or a lower-cost plan than Revé. Revé's listing, checked the same day, does not mention on-device storage, an account-free setup, or Apple Health. It requires a subscription for full access. Less of a fit if you need Android. The listing is headed "Only for iPhone" and does not offer Android.

Peptide Glow Up

Peptide Glow Up is a peptide tracker the App Store describes as built for women. The subtitle is "Cycle-aware peptide tracker." The listing says you log the peptides you take and a menstrual cycle in one place, including period start, period end, and confirmed ovulation, and that the app shows the current cycle phase. If you are not tracking a cycle, including if you are on hormonal birth control, the listing says those sections step aside. It also describes today's scheduled doses, weekly entries for weight, energy, sleep, skin, and libido on a 1 to 10 scale, up to three progress photos, a 30-second daily mood and symptom check-in, and side-effect notes. The listing says the app does not sell data, does not share it for advertising, and does not run third-party trackers in v1, and that you can delete your account in the app. It does not show a rating overview. The price is Free, and the listing does not show in-app purchases. It is offered for iPhone and iPad and requires iOS 15.1 or later. The listing does not mention GLP-1 medications, Android, or Apple Health, and it says the app does not recommend doses. Checked November 23, 2026.

Good fit if you want a peptide log listed as free, or you use an iPad. Revé's listing requires a subscription for full access and is iPhone only on iOS 17.0 or later. Less of a fit if you want GLP-1 medication tracking, Android, Apple Health, or no account. The listing does not say those things. Because it says you can delete an account in the app, it is not presented as account-free.

Revé

Revé is an iPhone app for women that logs peptides, supplements and GLP-1 routines with doses, timing, schedules and reminders, plus check-ins (mood, energy, sleep, appetite), measurements and progress photos. Every dose, check-in and measurement is tagged with cycle day and phase, so you can compare the same routine phase by phase and cycle to cycle. It's a free download, and full access needs a subscription.

Good fit if you take more than one thing and want to see whether it feels different in different weeks of your cycle. Less of a fit if you need food logging, Android, vial inventory, or a dosing calculator. Revé doesn't recommend doses.

What to check before you pick

  • Your medication or compound is supported, including compounded versions if that's what you use.
  • Cycle awareness. Does the app know your cycle day on each entry, or do you have to cross-reference a second app?
  • Side effects and check-ins are logged next to doses, not in a separate screen you'll forget.
  • Export for your clinician, such as a PDF or CSV.
  • Privacy: on-device only, or an account with cloud sync? Read the App Store privacy label.
  • Price: free tier limits and subscription cost.

Questions

Questions about choosing a tracker

What is the best GLP-1 tracker app for women?

If you only need injection and side-effect logs, Shotsy is the most widely used. If you want your menstrual cycle in the same timeline as your doses, look at apps built for women on GLP-1s, such as Back2Me and Steady, or Revé if you also track peptides or supplements.

Which tracker apps log both GLP-1 doses and the menstrual cycle?

Among the apps on this page, Back2Me, Steady, Protocol Her, and Revé say they track the menstrual cycle alongside GLP-1 doses. Revé tags every dose, check-in and measurement with cycle day and phase automatically.

Is a peptide 'cycle' the same as a menstrual cycle?

No. In peptide apps, a cycle usually means an on/off protocol schedule, such as weeks on and weeks off. A menstrual cycle runs from the first day of one period to the first day of the next. Many peptide trackers plan protocol cycles but don't know where you are in your menstrual cycle.

Is there a GLP-1 tracker app for Android?

Of the apps on this page, MeAgain lists both iPhone and Android. The others were reviewed from their iPhone App Store listings in November 2026; check Google Play for Android versions.

Do tracker apps give dosing advice?

Some peptide apps include dosing protocols or reconstitution calculators. Revé does not: it logs what you and your clinician decide and does not recommend doses.
