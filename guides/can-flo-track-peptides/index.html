Can Flo track peptides? No medication dose log

Checked Nov 28, 2026. What Flo logs for medication, and what a peptide tracker logs instead.

By the Revé editorial team · Updated Nov 28, 2026

Short answer

No, Flo has no peptide or medication dose log, and its medication log is birth control. The types are a pill, a ring, a patch, an injection, an IUD, or an implant. You turn on a reminder, and for the pill you mark it taken on time. Flo logs more than 80 symptoms and events, and predicts your period, ovulation, and fertile days. Revé logs the dose, the time, the schedule, a reminder, and the cycle phase on each entry.

Flo's column is from its help center. The medication log it describes is a birth control method. The free version also reminds you about your period, ovulation, birth control or medication, and a habit such as drinking water.

The peptide tracker column is Revé's US listing. You log peptides and supplements with a dose, a time, and a schedule, plus dose reminders. You can also record mood, energy, sleep, appetite, other changes you notice, and progress photos, and compare logs phase by phase and cycle to cycle.

Birth control reminders

From Flo's birth control article.

  1. Open Menu, then Reminders, then Medication and contraception.
  2. Choose the type you use, switch it on, make the edits, and save.
  3. To mark a pill, tap + on the main screen, then Oral contraceptives, then Taken on time, then Apply. You can log yesterday's pill the same way.

The types in that article are oral contraceptives, a vaginal ring, a contraceptive patch, an injection, an IUD, and an implant. Flo's glossary describes these reminders as taking a pill, changing a patch, ring, or implant, getting an injection, or checking the strings of an IUD. The injection in that list is a contraceptive injection. The article does not ask you to enter a dose.

You can turn medication, contraception, and lifestyle notifications on or off under Reminders.

Symptoms and categories

Flo's how to use the app article says there are more than 80 symptoms and events to log. The US listing says over 70 trackable symptoms and moods. You tap + on the main screen, select the symptoms or events, and apply them. To change the list, tap Edit next to Categories and switch a category off. The same edit is in Flo's article on predictions changing.

That edit switches Flo's own categories on or off. Logged items show on that date, and you can open the calendar to review a past day. Menu, then Graphs and reports, shows an overview of the current and past cycles. Symptom articles Flo publishes include menstrual flow, basal body temperature, and ovulation tests.

Some symptoms you enter can move a prediction. Flo says the more periods and symptoms you log, the more precise those predictions become. On the calendar, predicted period days, fertile days, and the ovulation day are marked in different colors. Stories in the app follow your cycle phase.

Questions

Questions about logging this in Flo

Can I log emergency contraception in Flo?

No. Flo's birth control article says that, at the moment, there is no option to log emergency contraception in the app.

Why did my fertile window disappear in Flo?

Once you turn on contraception reminders, Flo hides the fertile window and ovulation predictions. A copper IUD is the exception. Ovulation stays on the calendar as a gray circle. Flo's glossary says the app then treats you as using birth control and adjusts predictions and content.

Do I have to be 18 to log birth control in Flo?

Yes. Flo says logging that you have taken your birth control is available for users who are 18 or older.

What does Flo Premium add?

Cycle patterns, expert videos and articles, unlimited Health Assistant chats, a Symptom Checker, and on iPhone a doctor's report of the last six months that you can email or print. The Symptom Checker covers polycystic ovary syndrome and endometriosis. Flo's help article says it is not available in the United Kingdom or the European Union, and the US listing also excludes Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, and Switzerland.

Will Flo or Revé tell me what to take?

No. Flo's US listing says Flo does not provide medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment, and is not a method of birth control or conception aid. Flo's help center says the app is not intended to serve as a method of birth control or conception. Revé's US listing says Revé is a personal logging tool. It does not provide medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment, and it does not sell or supply any compound.
