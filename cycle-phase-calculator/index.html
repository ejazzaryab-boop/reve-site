What phase of my cycle am I in?

A free cycle phase calculator for a personal log. Enter the first day of your last period, a typical cycle length, and a typical period length. The result is a label you can write next to a note.

Read this first

These are estimates only. They use a calendar count from the dates and lengths you enter. This is not medical advice. It is not a contraceptive and it is not a fertility tool. It does not confirm ovulation. Irregular cycles, postpartum, perimenopause, hormonal contraception, or medications can change timing. Talk to a clinician about concerns.

Nothing you enter is saved or sent. Once this page has loaded, the count runs in this browser without a connection.

            01 / How the count works
            
    How the estimate works
    
            
    The count is simple on purpose. It does not read hormones, temperature, or tests. It only uses the three numbers you enter.
    
          
    
          
    
            
    
              01
              
    Day 1 is the date you enter
    
              
    Cycle day 1 is the first day of your last period. Today's cycle day counts forward from that date, and the start date itself is day 1.
    
            
    
            
    
              02
              
    Four labels share that length
    
              
    Menstrual is the period length you enter. Luteal is about the last 14 days of the cycle length, as a typical approximation, not a fixed rule. Ovulatory is a block of 3 days just before those luteal days. On this page that name is a calendar estimate. Follicular is the days left after bleeding and before that block. The page assigns these four calendar labels only when each one has its own days.
    
            
    
            
    
              03
              
    Today picks up one label
    
              
    If today falls inside the length and the four labels fit, today's calendar label is the one that contains that cycle day. If today is past the length, or the lengths do not leave room for all four labels, the page shows the cycle day and does not assign a label. It does not invent a new cycle.
    
            
    
          
    
        
    
      
    

      
    
        
    
          02 / Phases in order
          
    The four phases in order
    
          
    The names are Menstrual, Follicular, Ovulatory, and Luteal. They match the labels used elsewhere on this site. They are one name per day for a log. A physiology reference splits the cycle differently, and this page says so.
    
        
    
        
    
          
    
            
    Menstrual
    
            
    The first label. It covers the bleeding days you enter, from cycle day 1. A standard reference describes that bleeding as the shedding of the uterine lining, and it places the bleeding in the early days of the ovarian follicular phase.
    
          
    
          
    
            
    Follicular
    
            
    On this page, the days after the bleeding label and before the short ovulatory label. In the reference, the ovarian follicular phase starts on day 1 and runs until ovulation, so it is longer than this log label. That earlier part of the cycle is also the part that usually changes length when a cycle is longer or shorter.
    
          
    
          
    
            
    Ovulatory
    
            
    A short calendar label, a block of 3 days, placed just before the luteal days. The reference describes ovulation as a brief event, often about 14 days before the next bleeding in a typical cycle. That timing is a typical approximation, not a fixed rule. When today falls in these days, the result reads Ovulatory (calendar estimate). This label does not confirm that ovulation happened, and it is not for timing or avoiding pregnancy.
    
          
    
          
    
            
    Luteal
    
            
    The last label. It covers about the last 14 days of the cycle length you enter. The reference describes the luteal phase as the steadier part of the cycle, from ovulation until the next bleeding starts, often about 14 days. This page uses that figure as a typical approximation, not a fixed rule. If the lengths do not leave room for all four labels, the page does not assign one.
    
          
    
        
    
        
    This summary follows Physiology, Menstrual Cycle by Dhanalakshmi K. Thiyagarajan, Hajira Basit, and Rebecca Jeanmonod, in StatPearls, National Library of Medicine, updated September 27, 2024.
    
      
    

      
    
        
    
          03 / A personal log
          
    How to use phase labels in a log
    
        
    
        
    A phase label sits on a date so you can read that date next to the same part of a later cycle. It is for your own notes. It is not a reason to change what you take, what you eat, or how you spend the day.
    
        
    Copy or print the dates, then write the phase beside the matching day. A notebook, a spreadsheet, or the peptide and cycle log template all work. The template has a column for cycle phase. What to track in a peptide journal lists fields you may choose to write down, such as mood, energy, sleep, appetite, or another change you notice. Those are observations you choose to log. This page does not describe them as effects of a phase, or of anything you take.
    
        
    For measurements you choose to record, progress tracking by cycle phase is a printable grid lined up by the same four names.
    
        
    On iPhone, the app is Reve: Peptide & Cycle Tracker. You can log peptides and supplements with doses, timing, schedules, and reminders. Check-ins can record mood, energy, sleep, appetite, and changes you notice. Measurements and progress photos are tracked over time. Every dose, check-in, and measurement you log is tagged with your cycle phase, and you can compare those logs phase by phase and cycle to cycle. Progress photos are not given a phase tag. This page does not say supplement entries are given a phase tag. The app includes reference material in plain language. It does not tell you what to take, and it does not suggest a change based on cycle phase. This calculator does not connect to the app. The peptide and menstrual cycle tracker page describes that log.
    
      
    

      
    
        
    
          
    
            04 / Limits
            
    Limits of a calendar count
    
            
    One typical length cannot describe a cycle that changes from month to month. The luteal label uses about the last 14 days as a typical approximation, not a fixed rule. The ovulatory label is a block of 3 days in front of it, and it is a calendar estimate. Neither one is a test. The page does not confirm ovulation. If the lengths you enter do not leave room for Menstrual, Follicular, Ovulatory, and Luteal without overlap, the page shows the cycle day and does not assign a label.
    
            
    If today is past the length you entered, the estimate may be off. Update the start date if a new period has begun. The page will not decide that a new cycle started for you.
    
            
    Irregular cycles, postpartum, perimenopause, hormonal contraception, or medications can change timing. This page is not medical advice, not a contraceptive, and not a fertility tool. Talk to a clinician about concerns. Nothing here is a reason to change a routine, a dose, or what you do on a given day.

    Questions
    
            
    
              
    How does this cycle phase calculator estimate today's calendar label?
    
              
    It counts cycle day 1 as the first day you enter. When the lengths leave room for four calendar labels, Menstrual covers the period length you enter, Luteal covers about the last 14 days as a typical approximation, Ovulatory is a block of 3 days just before that, and Follicular is the days left between bleeding and that block. Today's calendar label is the name that contains today's cycle day. If the lengths do not leave room for all four, the page shows the cycle day and does not assign a label. The count runs in this browser.
    
            
    
            
    
              
    What are the menstrual cycle phases in order on this page?
    
              
    The labels in order are Menstrual, Follicular, Ovulatory, and Luteal. A physiology reference describes the ovarian cycle as a follicular phase from day 1 until ovulation, then a luteal phase until the next bleeding starts, with bleeding in the early follicular days. This page splits the days into four log labels. Those labels are not a hormone test.
    
            
    
            
    
              
    Can I use this as birth control or to plan a pregnancy?
    
              
    No. This is a calendar estimate for labeling personal notes. It is not a contraceptive and it is not a fertility tool. It does not confirm ovulation. Talk to a clinician about contraception, fertility, or any health concern.
    
            
    
            
    
              
    What if today is past the cycle length I entered?
    
              
    The page says the estimate may be off and asks you to update the start date if a new period has begun. It does not assign a calendar label for today, and it does not guess that a new cycle started.
    
            
    
            
    
              
    How do I put these labels on a personal log?
    
              
    Copy or print the phase dates, then write the phase next to the matching day in a notebook, a spreadsheet, or the peptide and cycle log template. The label is for reading your notes later. It is not a reason to change what you take or what you do.
